Photograph a stone, document its features, compare possible materials

1. Photograph the material

Field identification only; this version does not automatically recognize rock types from pixels. No photos leave your device. Do not scratch, wet, acid-test, clean or remove historic stone.

Aim at an exposed stone surface, with mineral texture visible; avoid relying on an inscription alone.

2. Geological map at the observation site

The mapped bedrock near a grave does not establish the quarry of a grave marker. This reports underlying mapped geology, not the material of imported stone.

Location is optional. You can enter latitude and longitude manually. Location is not saved or sent to Cemetery Picnic Club; the query goes to Macrostrat.

Source: Macrostrat geological map, CC BY 4.0, with original source references where provided. For Massachusetts, compare MassGIS Bedrock Lithology (coarse map, ~1.5 km estimated spatial accuracy) and USGS 1:24,000 surficial geology.

4. Possible materials

These are rule-based evidence matches, not AI predictions or calibrated probabilities. A photograph alone generally cannot identify an exact quarry. Quarry origin requires documented historical/geological comparisons.

Reference and provenance

Starter material profiles are descriptive field heuristics only, derived from the existing CPC Stone Material Identifier. No Mindat data or images are used. The optional geological lookup queries Macrostrat through our server only when requested. Original photos remain with you and are not included in the exported JSON.

Research sources: USGS · MassGIS Bedrock Lithology · Macrostrat. None of these sources is queried automatically in this version.
