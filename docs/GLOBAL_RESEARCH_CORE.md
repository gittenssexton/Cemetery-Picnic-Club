# Geographic Scope of the Research Core

Cemetery Picnic Club maintains **one globally scoped, evidence-linked research core**. No scholarly source, carving workshop, quarry, motif, burial custom, material, or historical person needs a connection to Pine Grove to belong in the core.

Pine Grove Cemetery in Warren, Massachusetts is the **first field pilot**, not the dataset boundary.

## Architecture rules

- Use permanent IDs for global entities and separately identify local cemetery/marker records.
- Index every claim by dated geographic coverage where the source supports it; coverage may be local, regional, transcontinental, global or unknown.
- Do not require a cemetery ID for makers, workshops, geological formations, quarries, artistic motifs, symbolic interpretations, sources or historical relationships.
- Permit cross-cemetery connections and many-to-many associations with documented evidence.
- Distinguish a symbol's *observable design* from its *scholarly interpretations*; each interpretation can be tied to time, tradition, location, author and source.
- Preserve aliases, historic place names, multiple writing systems, original-language terminology, and uncertain geolocation.
- Store data acquisition/photographic reuse rights separately from factual historical claims.
- Prefer shared global reference catalogs and controlled vocabularies to cemetery-specific copies of the same historical knowledge.
- Use Pine Grove markers to test app recognition and verification, never to filter or discard broad research.

## Research acquisition principle

Collect and catalog high-quality research on gravestones and funerary material culture wherever it is relevant, independent of geography. A source is useful if it improves the global evidence graph or our methods. Prioritize ingestion based on reliability, permissions, availability, and connection potential—not geographic proximity to Warren.

## Planned engine boundaries

Historical, scientific and image analysis, geology/provenance, craftsmanship, symbolism and iconography, family relationships, geography, and conservation. All engines share citations, provenance, confidence, review state, and change history.
